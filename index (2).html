02

Business challenges

  • Assumptions about newcomers need to be tested against audience evidence.
  • Aggregate results can hide differences in life stage, priorities and service needs.
  • The sample is concentrated in New Brunswick, which limits Atlantic-wide generalization.

Project objectives

05

Evidence & preparation

Survey preparation and descriptive analysis examined demographics, awareness and priority needs. The reported survey profile is 91% New Brunswick respondents, 77% aged 25–44, and 42% unaware of CAA. These describe the project sample, not the wider newcomer population.

07

Insight generation

  • 42% of respondents were unaware of CAA, indicating an awareness opportunity within the sample.
  • 77% were aged 25–44, making that life-stage group prominent in the research.
  • 91% were in New Brunswick, so broader Atlantic recommendations need further validation.
  • Affordable insurance was a leading priority, named by 77 respondents.

09

Recommendations

  • Explain CAA value in clear terms through newcomer-focused channels.
  • Test segment-specific offers around the needs identified in the research.
  • Validate findings with a broader Atlantic sample before scaling market assumptions.
10

Strategic recommendation

Use survey evidence to focus the value proposition, while testing the findings with a broader audience.

How success would be measured

  • Awareness by segment and geography
  • Service priorities and perceived value
  • Campaign engagement and membership conversion
  • Sample coverage across Atlantic provinces
