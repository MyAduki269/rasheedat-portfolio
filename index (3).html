01

Project overview

Company background

Dynamic Enterprise is a multinational FMCG and manufacturing organization with 75,000+ employees across five continents. Organic growth and independent regional technology choices have created a patchwork of HR systems.

Industry context

FMCG & manufacturing

Project background

How can a global organization create one connected HR experience while respecting regional needs?

Scope of the analysis

Current-state assessment, stakeholder engagement, requirements, future-state design, options evaluation and implementation planning.

Tools & technologies

PowerPoint

Business value

A decision framework for unified workforce visibility, consistent HR services and stronger governance.

Portfolio project summary

Connecting fragmented HR processes, workforce data and stakeholders through a phased global HCM transformation.

02

Business challenges

  • Employee records sit across legacy systems, cloud applications and spreadsheets.
  • Recruitment, payroll and performance tools follow different regional workflows.
  • Limited integration increases manual work, reporting effort and compliance complexity.
03

Business analysis

  • Current-state and gap analysis
  • Stakeholder needs and prioritized requirements
  • Future-state design and options evaluation
  • Scope, governance and RACI
  • Phased roadmap and stage gates
  • Change readiness, risk and benefits tracking
04

Project objectives

Define an integrated global HR operating model and a practical route from fragmented systems to a Global Integrated HCM platform.

05

Evidence & preparation

The case study describes the current HR landscape, stakeholder needs and operational issues. Requirements and solution options were assessed against these needs; financial values were not supplied.

06

Analysis & solution design

01

Understand the operating model

Mapped current-state fragmentation across people, process, data and technology.

02

Make stakeholder needs visible

Identified 17 stakeholder groups, prioritized engagement using a power–interest matrix and defined a tiered governance model for a wider stakeholder community.

03

Connect problems to requirements

Translated gaps into integrated HR capabilities, including master data, workflow automation, self-service and analytics.

04

Evaluate the route forward

Compared maintaining the current state, enhancing existing systems and implementing Global Integrated HCM.

07

Insight generation

  • Fragmentation is an operating-model problem spanning people, processes, data and technology.
  • The integrated HCM option offers the strongest strategic fit for unified data, consistent experience and governance.
  • Global design needs controlled local flexibility, regional representation and staged validation.
08

Reporting & deliverables

↗

Stakeholder analysis

17 groups, a power–interest matrix and tiered engagement.

↗

RACI & communications

18 activities mapped to roles, with channels, cadence and owners.

↗

Requirements & future state

An integrated HCM model spanning core HR, talent, self-service and analytics.

↗

Business case & roadmap

Options, cost categories, risks, benefits and a phased Hybrid Agile approach.

09

Recommendations

  • Establish shared data standards, process ownership and global requirements before selecting technology.
  • Pilot core HR, master data and integrations before progressing through regional rollout.
  • Use formal stage gates alongside iterative configuration, demonstrations and user feedback.
10

Strategic recommendation

Proceed with a phased Global Integrated HCM transformation using Hybrid Agile delivery.

How success would be measured

  • Employee-data accuracy and duplicate rate
  • Average HR transaction time
  • Workflow automation and self-service adoption
  • Privacy incidents and redundant systems retired
Evidence & outcome status

Proposed transformation. The case study establishes a future state and success measures; implementation results and financial returns are not reported.
